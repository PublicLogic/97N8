A FEW FACTS

Raised in Gardner

Master of Public Administration · Clark University

Gardner has always been personal to me.

My grandparents raised me in Gardner with a simple idea: take care of the place that takes care of you.

For me, that meant getting involved rather than standing on the sidelines. I served five terms on the Gardner City Council, representing Ward 3 from 2013 to 2022. I chaired the Public Service Committee and spent a lot of time dealing with the unglamorous things residents notice first: streets, public services, neighborhoods, budgets, and whether someone at City Hall will return a call.

That was my introduction to public office. It taught me to listen, answer directly, and remember that there is a real person on the other end of every city decision.

Then I went to work running towns.

I've worked in the Massachusetts State Senate, in a mayor's office, as Town Clerk in Princeton, and as Town Administrator in Hubbardston. My work now continues in municipal administration. I've had to build budgets, manage employees and contracts, find funding, and keep operations moving when the right answer isn't obvious.

I learned that a good idea doesn't mean much until you can pay for it, put someone in charge of it, and make it work. I also learned that people know when they're being given an answer and when they're being given the runaround.

Why think about another run?

I didn't leave Gardner behind when I left the Council. I stayed interested in the decisions being made here, and the years since have given me a much better understanding of what the executive side of government can—and can't—do.

I've been considering a return to elected office in Gardner. Mayor or City Council are both on the table, but I haven't chosen an office or announced a candidacy. That's where things stand.

There is plenty worth talking about before any decision: what we're spending, what we're maintaining, how businesses and families are doing, and whether City Hall is delivering what residents pay for.

I don't expect everyone to agree with me. I do expect a useful conversation.
